slv.measurements.mobile.transects#

TRAX transect matrices: obs[transect, point] over the 50-m network points.

Two generations of matrices:

  • Network matrices (this builder, 2026-09-24) — one matrix over the 50-m network points of load_trax_network_points() (1,397 points; shared track is one row of points), built from obs.parquet by build_network_transects(). Every transit of every line is a row, tagged with its line, direction and time, so pooling the lines is a concatenation and a source on the downtown trunk is scored once from all passes. Written per year by build_trax_transects() to $SLV_USER_DATA_DIR/trax/transects/network/ and read back by load_network_transects().

  • Archived per-line matrices (Logan Mitchell’s algorithm as rewritten in 2022; Dec 2014 – Apr 2023, single 9-s inlet lag, built from the duplicated-row parquet) — read by load_transects(). Provenance and cross-checks only.

The builder is Mitchell et al. (2018)’s algorithm on the generic pieces in lair.transects, with the TRAX specifics here:

  1. the on-track rows of obs.parquet (load_trax_obs(), so calibration provenance, the epoch offsets and the location classifier all apply);

  2. each sample is moved to where its air was taken in, lag_s earlier along the track (lair.transects.lag_positions()), with the lag of its instrument epoch (inlet_lag_seconds());

  3. samples snap to the nearest network point (60 m, like the STILT crossings);

  4. the line the train is on is resolved per sample by a vote among the letters of its own point: the line with the most single-line points hit within ±30 min (assign_lines());

  5. that line’s along-route coordinate is cut into one-way transits at reversals of travel and at gaps (lair.transects.split_transits()), and the samples are averaged onto [transit, point] with terminus dwells trimmed (lair.transects.transect_matrix()).

Coverage and short transits. Unlike the archived per-line matrices – built by chasing terminus polygons, so every row was (close to) a full end-to-end run – this builder keeps any one-way run of at least min_span_m, including short turns that never reach the line’s ends (a car shuttling Salt Lake Central <-> a few km out, real UTA service, ~15% of Red and ~29% of Green transits by span in the 2014-2026 record) and partial runs cut short by a mid-route reversal. Each transit’s coverage coordinate is its span over that line’s full known extent; a persistence metric that baselines a transit against its own low percentile (lair.transects.enhancement()) should filter on coverage first; a short loop that sits in a plume the whole way reads as background against itself. See sources/persistence/ for the filter in use.

slv.measurements.mobile.transects.assign_lines(point_lines, time_s, hit, window_s=1800.0, letters='RGBS')[source]#

The line letter each sample is on, resolved from the points around it in time.

point_lines is the lines string of each sample’s network point ("R", "BR", "BGR" on the trunk, …), time_s the sorted sample times (POSIX s) and hit whether the sample snapped to a point at all. Each sample is given the line, among the letters of its own point, with the most exclusive hits (samples on single-line points) within window_s before and after it. A sample on Red-only track is Red; a sample on the Red/Blue shared stretch is Red when the train was recently on Red-only track and Blue when it was recently on Blue-only track (e.g. a car in Blue service that just left the Blue-only stub at Salt Lake Central). The candidate restriction matters: a train that arrives from Green-only track and turns onto a Red/Blue-shared stretch must not carry G there, which a plain forward-fill did. Samples with no eligible vote (a point with no line tag, or half an hour on shared track with no exclusive point either side) come back "" and are left out of every line’s transits.

slv.measurements.mobile.transects.build_network_transects(obs, points, lag=None, max_point_dist=60.0, max_gap='10min', reversal_m=500.0, min_span_m=1000.0, max_dwell_s=180.0, species='CH4_ppm', return_samples=False)[source]#

Build the [transect, point] matrix of one obs frame on the network points.

Parameters:

  • obs (DataFrame) – Georeferenced samples with Time_UTC, Longitude_deg, Latitude_deg and the species column (obs.parquet rows; cal_source and low_pressure are summarised per transit when present). Any order; sorted here.

  • points (GeoDataFrame) – load_trax_network_points() (any CRS; needs point, lines, segment and the s_<line> columns).

  • lag (float | DataFrame | None) – Inlet lag: None, seconds, or the per-epoch table with start/end/lag_s (inlet_lag_seconds()).

  • max_point_dist (float) – Snap radius to a network point [m]; farther samples are off-network.

  • max_gap (str | Timedelta) – A transit (and a run for the line assignment and lag) ends at a longer gap.

  • reversal_m (float) – lair.transects.split_transits(): turning-point prominence that ends a transit, and the shortest along-route span kept.

  • min_span_m (float) – lair.transects.split_transits(): turning-point prominence that ends a transit, and the shortest along-route span kept.

  • max_dwell_s (float | None) – lair.transects.transect_matrix(): samples at a point later than this after the transit first reached it are dropped (terminus dwells).

  • return_samples (bool) – Also return the per-sample assignment (Time_UTC, x, y lagged UTM position, point (-1 off-network), line ("" unresolved), transit (-1 not in a kept transit)), for diagnostics.

Returns:

dims transect x point; variables obs (ppm), time (mean POSIX s of the samples in the cell) and n (samples); point coords point (network id), lon, lat, lines, segment, s_<line>; transect coords line, direction (+1 with increasing s), heading (compass letter of travel), t_start, t_end, s_min, s_max, n_fix, n_points (points with data), coverage ((s_max - s_min) over that line’s full known extent – a short turn or a partial run reads well below 1; see the module docstring), lag_s, lag_source, and frac_uncalibrated / frac_low_pressure when the tags are present. Attributes record every parameter.

Return type:

xarray.Dataset

Build and write the network matrices, one NetCDF per calendar year.

Each year is read from obs.parquet on its own (load_trax_obs(time_range=...), load_kwargs forwarded, default location on_track), built with build_network_transects() (lag and build_kwargs forwarded) and written to <transects_dir>/network/<site>_CH4_<year>.nc. Returns the files written; a year with no transits is skipped.
